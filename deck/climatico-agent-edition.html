The problem

For the companies growing fastest, this layer does not exist. Not badly — at all.

A twelve-person company whose emissions are overwhelmingly inference has three options, and none of them is a product they can use.

Option 01

Enterprise ESG suites

Priced, scoped and sold for organisations that already employ a sustainability lead. Seed-stage teams are not the customer and never were — they are told to come back at Series C.

wrong customer

Option 02

The cloud provider’s dashboard

One vendor’s slice, on that vendor’s methodology. No hardware, no vendors, no travel, no logistics — and no way to say which part of it belongs to which product line. Partial by construction.

one number, one vendor

Option 03

A consultant’s PDF

Twice a year, stale on arrival, reconstructed from invoices months after the activity. Nothing can query it, nothing can update it, and no system that caused the emissions ever sees it.

unqueryable

  • The gap is not a quality problem. Each of these does its own job competently. None of them is built for this customer, at this composition, on this cadence — and there is no fourth option to fall back on.
  • And the form now matters as much as the substance. The teams in this gap are building agent-native products and running their operations through agents. A layer that only a human can reach is a layer they will not use.

Substance — part two

And then the harder question: how much of it is yours?

Composition without attribution is trivia. The boundary — what this business is responsible for, versus what it merely touches — is where the honesty lives, and where every ESG tool quietly guesses.

Attribution 01

Scaled to this business, not a sector average

Defaults are per-FTE and scale to actual headcount; grid-linked activities are multiplied by the real hosting region and energy source. A ten-person team in a coal-heavy region does not get the same number as one on hydro.

Attribution 02

Every figure carries its own error bar

Uncertainty is per-activity and propagated in quadrature, so the total arrives as a range. Spend-based vendor estimates are ± 50% and are labelled as such — the model tells you which of its own numbers to distrust.

Attribution 03

Modeled is never printed as measured

Every default is tagged modeled until a utility bill, a billing export or a disposal record replaces it. The maturity ladder L0–L5 is literally the path from estimated to evidenced, one binding action per level.

Attribution 04

Avoided emissions start at zero

A handprint claim counts only once a conservative counterfactual baseline exists and additionality is demonstrated — the test that the reduction would not have happened anyway. Until then the number is 0, not a marketing figure.

Attribution 05

Netting is described, never sold

Footprint and handprint use separate accounting baselines. Subtracting one from the other is descriptive and is labelled that way; it is not an offset, and calling it one is the claim we refuse.

Attribution 06

Efficiency gains are discounted for rebound

Making inference cheaper lowers the price of running more of it. The Jevons rebound is flagged against every compute-efficiency claim, with the UKERC evidence base attached, instead of being counted as a clean win.

Why it has to be an agent interface

Attribution is a moment-of-activity problem. Forms arrive months late.

Where the answer actually lives

“How much of this is ours” is answerable at exactly one moment: when the spend happens, in a known region, against a known product line. An hour later it is an invoice line; a quarter later it is a reconstruction.

The systems standing at that moment are not people. They are the agents already watching the cloud bill, approving the vendor, booking the travel, running the inference.

Where these companies are moving

The teams in this gap are building agent-native products and wiring their own operations to agents. Their interface convention is a card, a scoped token, a tool call — not a login and a six-step form.

An impact layer they have to visit is a layer they will visit twice a year, under duress. One their agents can call is a layer that gets a record every time something happens.

So the shape is

A write surface, not a dashboard

Discovery card → self-minted scoped credential → grounded write → durable receipt. The screen is the optional part.

Which forces

Policy that runs before the write

If a machine can file a claim, the boundary rules have to be executable, not printed in a methodology appendix nobody reads.

And gives us

Provenance attached at the source

Evidence, subject, scope and timestamp travel with the claim from the moment it is made, instead of being reconstructed for a disclosure cycle two years later.

The one rule

A refusal is a receipt.

Both of these are rows in the same SQLite table, with the same shape, the same id, and the same permanence. That symmetry is what makes an unattended write surface safe to hand a stranger.

How to use it — cold start

Five calls from a URL to a durable write.

No signup, no dashboard, no API key emailed to a human. The credential is minted by the caller, for the caller, with the ceiling baked in.

01
Read the card
GET /.well-known/agent-card.json — name, skills, auth scheme, and the MCP, credential and policy URLs.
02
Mint your own credential
POST /v1/credentials with a subject and the scopes you want. Scopes freeze at mint; the offset ceiling comes with them.
03
Carry the bearer
The same token authenticates /mcp, /a2a and /v1/*. Unauthenticated writes get 401 before any work happens.
04
Write
MCP complete_action, REST POST /v1/actions, or A2A SendMessage. Pick your protocol; the policy is the same.
05
Keep the receipt
A UUID that resolves for as long as the object lives. Retry with the same idempotencyKey and you get the original row back, not a second commit.
Mint 
# nothing but the base URL is needed
curl -X POST $BASE/v1/credentials \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"subject":"judge-agent",
       "scopes":["climatico:read",
                 "climatico:transact"]}'
Write 
curl -X POST $BASE/v1/actions \
  -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"intent":"brief",
       "location":"Houston, TX"}'
# → 201, receipt with 5 cited sources

Attribution at the moment it happens, not at the quarter’s end.

This is the whole argument compressed into one trigger: a spike is caught where it occurs, assigned to a region and a business, scored against that business’s own budget, and settled — with every step written down as it is decided.

Client case 02 — hardware

A client who makes a physical thing is not the same client at 10× the number.

Our second client type builds devices. Almost none of their footprint is inference, their hotspot sits in a factory they do not own, and a peer range borrowed from SaaS would be off by an order of magnitude.

Client case 02 — their workflow

Their emissions are created by purchase orders and freight bookings, so that is what gets filed.

The compute client’s trigger is a billing spike. This client’s triggers are operational decisions made by people and agents in ops, procurement and logistics — each one a moment where attribution is still cheap.

01
A supplier PO is cut
The ops agent files component class, supplier region and spend as it happens. This lands in Scope 3 Cat 1 or Cat 2 the day the commitment is made — not in a reconstruction from invoices next March.
02
A shipment is booked
Tonne-kilometres and mode, scored on the GLEC Framework / ISO 14083. When a delayed line forces sea → air, the agent files the mode change at the moment of the decision, while someone can still weigh it.
03
A prototype or assembly run completes
Scrap, packaging and e-waste against a modeled 250 kg-per-FTE facility default — explicitly provisional, and replaced the moment real disposal records exist.
04
A meter is read, a refrigerant is topped up
Scope 1 and Scope 2 come back for anyone with a building. Utility kWh and fugitive refrigerant convert two whole classes from modeled to measured in one write.
05
An enterprise buyer asks for supplier data
Their customer’s procurement agent gets a climatico:read token and pulls receipts with sources attached. It can read what was committed; it cannot see the bill of materials, and it cannot write.
06
The handprint claim goes in the deck
“Our device displaces a dirtier one.” Refused as ungrounded until a conservative counterfactual baseline exists and additionality is demonstrated. The refusal is stored, with the reason, so the next attempt starts from it.
Client case 02 — what they weigh

Six things this client worries about that a compute client never mentions.

Concern 01

The peer range is an order of magnitude off

Benchmarked against SaaS they look catastrophic; benchmarked against nothing they look fine. 15–45 against 1.5–4.0 tCO2e per FTE is not a rounding difference, and a tool that does not know which one they are is worse than no tool.

Concern 02

Procurement sets the deadline, not the regulator

The request arrives from an enterprise customer pulling suppliers into its own value-chain reporting — on the buyer’s timeline, in the buyer’s format. For a hardware startup selling into large accounts, the footprint becomes a condition of the sale.

Concern 03

The hotspot is in a factory they do not own

Most of the number sits with a contract manufacturer they cannot meter. Until supplier-specific factors arrive, the fallback is a spend-based proxy at ± 50% — usable, but only if the tool says out loud how coarse it is.

Concern 04

Freight mode is the live lever

A single air expedite can outweigh a year of office electricity. It is decided mid-week under schedule pressure by someone who will never open a sustainability dashboard — which is precisely the argument for an interface their systems can call.

Concern 05

The boundary has to be declared, not implied

Cradle-to-gate and cradle-to-grave give different numbers for the same device. At L3 the top hotspot needs a real ISO 14040/14044 or GHG Protocol Product Standard lifecycle assessment — and a spend-based estimate must never be allowed to describe itself as one.

Concern 06

Water, waste and land stop being footnotes

Scope 1 combustion and refrigerant leakage return, prototyping and assembly waste is material, and land & biodiversity rises from negligible to a medium materiality flag on raw-material extraction. Carbon alone stops describing the business.

And the one that shapes the architecture

The bill of materials is the most confidential thing they own

Supplier identities and part costs are the crown jewels — in our founder sessions, privacy drew the strongest reaction of any topic, and stealth-stage teams refused document upload outright. Scoped credentials are the answer we already ship: a supplier’s agent can be given a token that writes one activity and reads nothing, and a buyer’s agent one that reads receipts and writes nothing. Scopes freeze at mint, so neither can widen its own reach.

What holds it together

The boring parts are the demo.

Credentials

Scopes freeze at mint

HMAC-SHA256 over a payload the caller cannot edit; TTL clamped to 24 hours; every token recorded by hash so it can be revoked. climatico:read can look but never write. Thirty mints a minute, then a 429.

Money

A ceiling the caller can’t raise

A standard token commits at most 5 000¢. Asking for more is amount_exceeds_scope, refused and stored. The fleet caps its own settlement at the same ceiling rather than splitting the difference.

Truth

Grounded, or it does not exist

A brief or assessment with no live sources behind it comes back ungrounded. We would rather return nothing than a plausible climate paragraph. The audit refuses to score emissions on an unverifiable factor.

Replays

Retry is safe

An idempotencyKey returns the original receipt instead of committing twice. Agents retry; the ledger should not double-spend because of it.

Memory

Nothing lives in RAM

Receipts, credentials, watches, handoffs and fleet runs are SQLite rows inside a Durable Object. A watch opened during the demo is still there tomorrow.

Integrity

No decorative integrations

Every dependency in the write path does real work: Workers, Durable Objects, Workers AI, Tavily. Nothing is called to put a logo on a slide, and nothing is stubbed to look busy.
